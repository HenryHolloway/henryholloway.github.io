Overview

Welcome to the Secondary Mathematics Teaching Toolkit. This resource is designed to support educators in creating inclusive, engaging, and effective mathematics learning experiences for students in grades 6-12.

Introduction

This toolkit serves as a comprehensive resource for secondary mathematics educators, providing practical strategies, materials, and technological tools to enhance teaching and learning. Developed as part of EDU563, this project synthesizes current research in mathematics education, Universal Design for Learning (UDL), and culturally responsive teaching.

The toolkit is designed to be a living document, adaptable to various classroom contexts and student needs. It emphasizes evidence-based practices that promote conceptual understanding, procedural fluency, and mathematical reasoning for all learners.

Target Audience

This toolkit is specifically designed for:

  • Grade Levels: Secondary mathematics (Grades 6-12)
  • Subject Areas: Algebra, Geometry, Algebra II, Pre-Calculus, Calculus, Statistics
  • Educators: Pre-service and in-service mathematics teachers
  • Special Populations: Students with diverse learning needs, multilingual learners, gifted students

While designed for secondary educators, many strategies and resources could be adapted for elementary or post-secondary contexts.

Purpose & Context

The purpose of this toolkit is to provide mathematics educators with:

  • Practical, research-based instructional strategies
  • Diverse assessment methods that reveal student understanding
  • Technology tools that enhance mathematical learning
  • UDL and differentiation strategies for diverse learners
  • Resources for culturally responsive mathematics teaching

This toolkit addresses the need for comprehensive, accessible resources that support educators in implementing equitable mathematics instruction. It is grounded in the belief that all students can succeed in mathematics when provided with appropriate support, challenges, and learning opportunities.

Teaching Strategies

Identification and elaboration of teaching strategies specifically tailored to secondary mathematics.

Spacing and Retrieval

Retention Strategy

Proper memorization of mathematical processes are important within mathematics. An effective method to have students better retain information is through the practices of spacing and retrieval. Spacing is the process of having students continuously practice a topic but having the distance in time between these practices become larger. Upon learning a mathematic process, practice it again the next day, then again a few days later, and again many more days later. This repetition helps further cement the mathematical processes within students' minds. With this spaced practice, students will also become better at retrieving the information they need to complete the mathematical process. Though, having students practice it on their own time would also greatly improve their ability to pull and use the knowledge they have built up.

White board practice

Engagement

White board practice is a helpful way to learn mathematics. Through white boards, students can work with their peers, work by themselves, or follow along with the class. With mathematics, it is especially helpful for students, since students can work through problems and be able to correct issues they make quickly. Additionally, white boards are more exciting to use, making for greater engagement from students. With white boards, practicing mathematics can happen in a multitude of ways, they can complete problems given, they can answer questions, they can write down thoughts, so many different ways to use white boards.

Gamification

Student Motivation

One way to encourage engagement from students is to present learning as a game. Mathematics is a topic that tends to get push back from students, so trying to make it as engaging as possible is one way to better communicate it to students. Through gamification, the act of learning becomes more fun and engaging. There are many ways to gamify mathematics, and in the resource we have listed, it describes several different ways to turn mathematics into a game. Through playing these games, students would not only be more willing to engage with mathematics, but they would also be able to connect with their peers more, creating a more cohesive classroom.

Assessment Strategies

Presentation of diverse formative assessment methods appropriate for secondary mathematics.

Warmups

Formative Assessment

Warmups are a small but helpful way to assess student knowledge. The link listed shows 8 different warmups to try with students. Some are shorter activities, taking up to 5 minutes of time. While others are longer activities, taking up much more time depending on what knowledge is wished to be gone over or practiced. Warmups can prepare students for what is to be learned today, to practice what they learned the day prior, or to just practice mathematics. Using warmups as an assessment is a quick and easy way to measure student understanding on the topic that the warmup is based around. As it quickly shows whether a student grasps the concept or not.

Resource Link

Exit tickets

Formative Assessment

Exit tickets in math are a short, helpful assessment to measure student understanding and see how comfortable they are with a concept. Exit tickets can be as simple as asking whether a student is understanding a topic, or as long as having them solve a problem. Exit tickets are also a great way to ensure that students are busy until the end of class, as it gives them a short but important activity to do before leaving class for the day.

Resource Link

Effective Feedback

Growth & Reflection

A part of any effective assessment is proper feedback, especially within mathematics. Feedback can be given out in multiple ways, be it through providing feedback directly to the student on their quiz, sending them an email, or just discussing the assessment with the whole class. Feedback is especially important within mathematics, as feedback functions as a way to clear up misunderstandings, or help students understand what mistake they made. With mathematics, there are so many areas within a problem to make a mistake, ensuring that students recognize where that mistake was made, and why it is considered a mistake is important for proper growth among students.

UDL & Differentiation Strategies

Specific strategies grounded in Universal Design for Learning and differentiation for diverse mathematics learners.

Multiple Means of Engagement

Strategies to optimize learner motivation, interest, and sustained effort in mathematics learning.

Recruiting Interest

  • Provide mathematical problems that connect to students' lives and cultures
  • Offer choices in topics, contexts, or methods for exploration
  • Use authentic, real-world mathematical tasks and projects
  • Incorporate mathematical games and puzzles

Sustaining Effort & Persistence

  • Provide clear mathematical goals and objectives
  • Use collaborative mathematical problem-solving
  • Offer varied levels of challenge and support
  • Provide opportunities for mathematical communication and reasoning

Self-Regulation

  • Teach mathematical metacognitive strategies
  • Provide opportunities for self-assessment and reflection
  • Develop mathematical identity and agency
  • Encourage mathematical risk-taking and productive struggle

Multiple Means of Representation

Strategies to present mathematical concepts and information in diverse ways.

Perception

  • Provide mathematical information in multiple formats (text, symbols, diagrams, manipulatives)
  • Offer auditory and visual alternatives for mathematical content
  • Use mathematical software that allows customization of display
  • Provide mathematical vocabulary support

Language & Symbols

  • Clarify mathematical notation and symbols
  • Provide mathematical glossaries and reference materials
  • Use mathematical representations (graphs, tables, diagrams)
  • Connect mathematical symbols to concepts

Comprehension

  • Use mathematical modeling and multiple representations
  • Provide mathematical examples and non-examples
  • Use visualizations and interactive tools
  • Connect mathematical concepts across topics

Multiple Means of Action & Expression

Strategies to differentiate how learners demonstrate mathematical understanding.

Physical Action

  • Provide mathematical tools and manipulatives
  • Offer mathematical software and technology
  • Use mathematical drawing and construction tools
  • Provide mathematical templates and organizers

Expression & Communication

  • Allow multiple ways to demonstrate mathematical understanding
  • Use mathematical writing and speaking opportunities
  • Provide mathematical modeling and problem-solving
  • Use mathematical presentations and explanations

Executive Function

  • Provide mathematical goal-setting and planning support
  • Use mathematical information management tools
  • Provide mathematical progress monitoring
  • Teach mathematical self-monitoring strategies

Differentiation Strategies for Diverse Learners

Students with Disabilities

  • Conceptual Understanding: Use concrete manipulatives and visual representations
  • Procedural Fluency: Provide step-by-step worked examples and practice
  • Problem Solving: Offer graphic organizers and problem-solving frameworks
  • Assessment: Allow alternative response formats and extended time
  • Technology: Use mathematical software with accessibility features

Multilingual Learners

  • Language Support: Provide mathematical vocabulary lists with translations
  • Visual Aids: Use diagrams, charts, and manipulatives
  • Sentence Frames: Provide mathematical communication starters
  • Cultural Connections: Include mathematical contributions from diverse cultures
  • Grouping: Use heterogeneous and homogeneous language groups

Gifted & Talented Students

  • Depth: Explore mathematical concepts in greater depth
  • Complexity: Use more complex mathematical problems and contexts
  • Pace: Allow acceleration through mathematical content
  • Creativity: Encourage mathematical creativity and exploration
  • Independence: Provide opportunities for independent mathematical research

Educational Technology Tools

Clear, concise descriptions of technology tools for enhancing mathematics learning experiences, including concrete examples of effective classroom integration.

Blooket

Gamified Learning Formative Assessment

Blooket is a gamified, web-based learning platform that allows teachers to create or discover question sets and host them in interactive, game-based modes. For teachers, it serves as a formative assessment and review tool, while for students, it provides an engaging, competitive, and interactive way to practice concepts.

Classroom Integration: Compared to a traditional instructional approach, such as handing out a printed Geometry review worksheet, Blooket enhances the learning experience by providing immediate feedback. If a student misidentifies a supplementary angle, the game immediately corrects them, allowing for real-time self-correction rather than waiting for a teacher to grade the paper later. The free version is highly robust. Students do not need to log in or have email accounts to play; they simply enter a game PIN or scan a QR code on their devices, which is highly beneficial for student privacy and equitable access.

UDL & Culturally Responsive Teaching: Blooket’s development is rooted in the principles of gamification and game-based learning, which educational research shows can significantly increase student motivation and information retention. Grounding Blooket in the principles of Universal Design for Learning, it provides multiple means of engagement by offering various game modes; teachers can select fast-paced competitive modes or self-paced modes like "Classic" to accommodate different learner preferences and anxiety levels. Regarding culturally responsive teaching, Blooket allows teachers to custom-build questions that incorporate students' identities, cultures, and lived experiences. For example, a Geometry teacher can use word problems that reference local community architecture or culturally significant geometric patterns.

Generative AI Integration: Generative AI tools like ChatGPT or Gemini can significantly impact the use of Blooket. A teacher can leverage AI to instantly generate Geometry word problems formatted for Blooket, or to brainstorm culturally diverse contexts for math questions. However, the ethical considerations include the risk of AI hallucinating incorrect math formulas, the environmental cost of AI, and the ethical dilemma of teachers over-relying on AI rather than crafting their own pedagogically sound questions.

GeoGebra

Dynamic Math Interactive

Geogebra is an online website that is a “dynamic mathematics software for all levels of education”. (GeoGebra, 2025) The website has two main aspects, their calculator and their resources. Their calculator section is applicable to geometry, algebra, spreadsheets, graphing, statistics, and calculus. Their resources offer over 1 million classroom resources created by both their staff and their general online community. They offer the ability to host a GeoGebra Classroom where student progress can be monitored as well as the ability to use their resources for free and without any logins.

A teacher may choose to use this online resource as simply a calculator for themself or their students use, to assign activities to extend their student learning, or to generate graphs for their own assignments or tests. What led me to investigate this application is my desire to learn more about the platform and the different uses as my mentor teacher utilizes this online resource more than any other in her geometry and algebra classrooms at the high school level. GeoGebra can enhance the learning experience of the students by offering interactive activities that allow the student to approach the mathematical content beyond a set of rules to learn, or formulas to memorize, but to truly understand and visualize the concepts. This can support culturally responsive teaching by supporting Universal Design for Learning practices by offering different ways of teaching and learning for students. GeoGebra is an international website that offers nearly 40 languages you can choose from to view the platform in providing support to many different diverse learners.

Wayground (formerly Quizizz)

Gamified Learning Data & Analytics

Wayground is an online web browser gamified learning platform. This website allows teachers to find or create assignments for students to then engage with. Students can engage with these assignments through quiz style questions, which can be done individually, or as a class. Where the website differs from others is through its gamification of these assignments. There are several different games that can be played, and within all of these games are tools that are helpful to teachers. For every student, it provides personalized data for each question the student answers. The app is not only engaging to students but also provides helpful information to teachers.

Wayground is unique in that a classroom can be imported into the website, meaning that students who have signed up will have individualized settings and statistics. These settings are especially helpful for students who need additional assistance, as the settings are tools such as a dictionary, automatic translation, additional time on questions, hints that the teacher writes, screen reading, calculators, and so many more. With these settings being set for individual students, it allows for students to be able to get additional assistance without having to go through additional hoops. Setting it up once when the classroom is created is all that is needed, and the settings will persist for the rest of the year.

Teachers benefit from using this website through the data that is given from student participation. Each answer that a student gives is remembered and returned to the teacher with information such as how long it took them to complete the question, how many times did they alt-tab, did they get the question right, how many questions did they get wrong, what areas they struggled the most with, and more. This information can then be used to provide feedback on areas of improvement. As it directly highlights what questions students are struggling with, and what questions students are showing confidence in. Through Waygrounds gamified assignments, it further encourages engagement through incentivizing students by competitiveness. With there being a scoreboard, it provides students with a want to do better on the assignment so they can be on the podium, though the scoreboard can be disabled. Waygrounds has more than one style of assignment, with some of the assignment being continuous questions over a set period of time, or a set number of questions with a set amount of try on each. Getting a question incorrect will notify the student of the correct answer, and teachers can choose to write more down as feedback if they choose too. Wayground provides students with an immediate response on the questions they submit, which assists students in recognizing if they are understanding mathematical processes.
